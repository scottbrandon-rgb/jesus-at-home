# MAIN IDEA

- Grumbling doesn't change our circumstances. It changes our sight.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS

- Pastor Caleb talked about 4 ways a grumbling spirit changes our sight, using the story of the Israelites in the wilderness:

	➡ 1. **It Keeps You Looking Back Instead of Looking at What God Is Doing Now** (Exodus 16:1-3; Numbers 11:4-5)
	- Israel didn't have an appetite for what God was doing right in front of them because they were still craving what was behind them.
	- Your attitude affects your appetite!
	- If you catch yourself craving the wrong things, you might need to check your attitude and what you're feeding your spirit.

	➡ 2. **It Causes You To Minimize Your Blessings** (Numbers 11:6; Numbers 21:5)
	- A grumbling spirit causes you to downplay your blessings.
	- The Israelites treated a miracle like it was nothing.

	➡ 3. **It Causes You To Magnify Your Problems** (Numbers 13:31-33)
	- A grumbling spirit makes your problems look bigger than your God.
	- Joshua & Caleb saw the same land and same giants and they said, "The Lord is with us; do not be afraid!" Same circumstances, different sight.

	➡ 4. **It Distorts How You See God** (Deuteronomy 1:27)
	- A grumbling spirit distorts how we see God in 3 ways:
		**A.** It questions His heart ("If God really loved me, He wouldn't let this happen.")
		**B.** It questions His presence (Exodus 17:7)
		**C.** It questions His power (Psalm 78:19-20)

## THIS WEEK’S QUICK WIN

- Ask: What’s something you’ve caught yourself grumbling or complaining about lately?
- Follow up with: What’s one blessing in that same situation that you might be overlooking?
- Read Philippians 2:14-15 again.
- Pray together and ask God to give you a heart of gratitude instead of a grumbling spirit.

## FURTHER DISCUSSION

- Share a high and a low from your week.
- Have someone restate Philippians 2:14-15 in their own words.
- This week we talked about how grumbling doesn’t fix our problems — it changes the way we see everything. In the wilderness, God was providing manna every morning, water from a rock, and His presence leading them, yet the Israelites grumbled until they convinced themselves God didn’t care about them.

**Then ask:**
- Why do you think the Israelites missed the miracle that showed up at their door every morning?
- Pastor Caleb said, “Your attitude affects your appetite.” What do you think he meant by that?
- Joshua and Caleb saw the same giants as everyone else. What made their perspective different?
- What are some things in our home, school, or church that are easy to grumble about but are actually blessings?

## WHAT THIS SHOWS US ABOUT GOD

- **God is a faithful provider.** Even when His people grumbled, He kept sending manna every single morning.
- **God had not changed — their sight changed.** Our complaining doesn’t change who God is, but it can change how we see Him.
- **God is bigger than our giants.** When we measure our problems against Him instead of against ourselves, we can say like Joshua and Caleb, “The Lord is with us; do not be afraid!”

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR US

 - Pastor Caleb shared a few ways we can change our sight:

	➡ **Watch Your Intake** 
    - Matthew 6:22–23 says the eye is the lamp of the body. Attitude affects intake, and what we take in is what comes out. If our intake is negative and critical, our output will be too.
	➡ **Feed On The Fresh Things of God** 
	- John Bevere says, “You hunger for what you feed on.” If we don’t crave the things of God, we may need to check what we’re feeding our spirit.
	➡ **Dwell On What Is Good** 
    - Philippians 4:8 tells us to think on whatever is true, honorable, just, pure, lovely, and commendable.
	➡ **Set Your Mind On Things Above** 
    - Colossians 3:1–2 reminds us to keep our focus on Christ, not just on what’s happening around us.
	➡ **Repent** 
    - Repentance changes your sight. When we turn from a grumbling spirit, God creates a clean heart and renews a right spirit in us (Psalm 51:10).

**Talk together about this:**
- What would change in our home if we chose gratitude over grumbling this month?
